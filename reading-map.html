Guiding texts and weekly reading map

The course draws on two guiding texts, but it does not force a textbook citation into every explanation. This page records where each text directly supports the teaching spine and where the MasterClass moves into computational workflows or dynamic extensions developed from other cited sources.

  • Edelsbrunner and Harer, Computational Topology supplies the computational-complex and persistent-homology spine (Edelsbrunner and Harer 2010).
  • Postol, Algebraic Topology supplies a more traditional route through the algebraic-topology foundations (Postol 2024).

How to use the map

Preparatory means background that can be consulted before the week. Core companion means the chapter closely supports the week’s main argument. Supplementary means it offers another route but is not required to follow the course.

The research papers listed below are optional. They are routes into provenance, applications or advanced frameworks, not additional required weekly reading.

Course material Role of the guiding texts
Topology survival guide No single assigned passage. The guide supplies only the point-set topology needed to read Week 1.
Week 1: shape and topology Conceptual motivation assembled for the dynamical systems audience; neither guiding text is treated as a required opening chapter.
Algebra survival guide Preparatory: Postol, Chapter 3. Edelsbrunner and Harer, Chapter IV is the later destination for the matrix language, not the source of the first-year refresher.
Week 2: simplicial complexes and homology Core companions: Edelsbrunner and Harer, Chapters III.1 and IV; Postol, Section 4.1.
Week 3: filtrations Core companion: Edelsbrunner and Harer, Chapter III, especially III.2 for Čech and Rips. Supplementary: Postol, Chapter 5.
Week 4: persistent homology Core companion: Edelsbrunner and Harer, Chapters VII–VIII. Supplementary: Postol, Chapter 5.
Week 5: static workflows The guiding texts support the underlying complexes and persistence. The software workflows use the sources and documentation cited on the weekly page and in the notebooks.
Week 6: summaries and validation The persistence foundations remain relevant, but the comparison summaries, surrogate design and validation argument use the sources cited on the weekly page.
Week 7: repeated persistence through time This dynamic workflow extends beyond the main coverage of the two guiding texts. Follow the research references attached to the constructions and examples.
Week 8: advanced dynamic frameworks Zigzag, multiparameter, vineyard and vineyard-module material requires the research literature cited in Week 8; the two guiding texts remain background rather than direct companions.

Optional papers and data provenance

Course connection Optional source Why it is here
Week 1 notebook data Matejka and Fitzmaurice, “Same Stats, Different Graphs” (Matejka and Fitzmaurice 2017), together with the datasauRus data-package record (Gillespie et al. 2025) Provenance for the included Datasaurus Dozen CSV and the construction that holds familiar summaries approximately fixed while changing visible organisation. This is the only externally sourced dataset currently shipped with the notebooks.
Week 7, dynamic aggregation Topaz, Ziegelmeier and Halverson, “Topological Data Analysis of Biological Aggregation Models” (Topaz et al. 2015) A dynamical systems application using simulation frames, Betti numbers across time and scale, and comparisons with conventional order parameters. The Week 7 notebook uses its own small synthetic teaching example rather than reproducing these simulation data.
Week 8, zigzag persistence Carlsson and de Silva, “Zigzag Persistence” (Carlsson and Silva 2010) The foundational route for persistence when maps legitimately point in both directions because spaces or representations grow and shrink.
Week 8, vineyards Cohen-Steiner, Edelsbrunner and Morozov, “Vines and Vineyards by Updating Persistence in Linear Time” (Cohen-Steiner et al. 2006) Introduces vineyards for a continuously changing family of persistence diagrams and includes a protein-folding application.
Week 8, multiparameter persistence Carlsson and Zomorodian, “The Theory of Multidimensional Persistence” (Carlsson and Zomorodian 2009) Explains why several genuine filtration parameters require a different algebraic framework and why an ordinary one-dimensional barcode is no longer a complete invariant.
Week 8, vineyard modules Turner, “Representing Vineyard Modules” (Turner 2023) The course’s direct source for the extra algebraic information retained by vineyard modules beyond visible vines.
Week 4, persistence and stability Edelsbrunner, Letscher and Zomorodian (Edelsbrunner et al. 2002) and Zomorodian and Carlsson (Zomorodian and Carlsson 2005) The foundational filtration, algorithm and algebraic-classification route that predates the stability theorem. Cohen-Steiner, Edelsbrunner and Harer (Cohen-Steiner et al. 2007) then give the original bottleneck-stability theorem for tame functions on a common space. Chazal, de Silva and Oudot (Chazal et al. 2014) provide the separate geometric-complex route relevant to Rips and Čech filtrations on metric data.

Citation convention

The main notes cite a chapter where a definition, theorem or computational convention is used directly. Broader source orientation remains on this page so that the teaching prose can focus on explanation. Notebooks cite a source only when it clarifies a convention or the provenance of an implemented construction.

Candidate public datasets for Weeks 5–8

These datasets are not yet bundled with the course. They are a research shortlist for later practicals. Any adopted dataset should be reduced to a documented teaching subset, with its licence and provenance retained beside the derived file.

Candidate Natural course use Baseline and modelling questions Practical assessment
Fish-school collective U-turn trajectories Weeks 5, 7 and 8. Construct position clouds or interaction graphs at each frame; compare repeated persistence through time; investigate growing and shrinking neighbourhood relations as a possible zigzag problem. Compare topology with speed, polarisation, nearest-neighbour distance and the recorded U-turn times. Decide whether positions, headings or an interaction graph are the scientific object of analysis. Best match to the dynamical and complex systems audience. Dryad provides group-size-specific downloads, but the full collection is about 740 MB. Begin with one short trial from a small group and document identity gaps and coordinate conventions.
MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database Weeks 5–7. Build delay-coordinate clouds from short ECG windows; compare persistence summaries between rhythms or through annotated transitions. Compare with heart rate, spectral power and established beat annotations. Vary delay, embedding dimension, window length and noise handling. Strong, well-documented signal example: 48 two-channel half-hour records, beat annotations and an Open Data Commons Attribution licence. Use a few short, non-diagnostic excerpts and state that the exercise makes no clinical claim.
NASA IMS bearing degradation data Weeks 6 and 7. Follow vibration-window representations and persistence summaries over a run-to-failure experiment. Compare with RMS vibration, kurtosis and spectral features. Test whether a topological summary changes before or merely alongside conventional condition indicators. Strong industry route with public access and US-government reuse terms. The raw vibration archive requires windowing and careful control for operating condition, so provide a small pre-indexed subset rather than the full archive.
UCI Human Activity Recognition using Smartphones Weeks 5–7. Compare point-cloud representations of accelerometer and gyroscope windows across six activities or follow topology across postural transitions. Compare with classification, clustering, covariance and spectral baselines. Contrast raw inertial signals with the supplied 561-feature representation and avoid treating overlapping windows as independent observations. Accessible and CC BY 4.0, with 30 participants and clearly labelled activities. The full download is about 58 MB. Subject-level splitting is essential to avoid leakage.
UCI Air Quality sensor data Weeks 5–8. Study multivariate sensor-state clouds, drift through time, or two-parameter questions involving neighbourhood scale and time or environmental conditions. Compare with PCA, regression residuals, seasonality and drift diagnostics. Missing values coded as \(-200\) must be removed before any distance calculation. Technically convenient at under 2 MB, with 9,358 hourly observations. The repository page displays CC BY 4.0 metadata but also older research-only wording, so resolve the usage terms before redistributing a derived course file.

Recommended development order. Prototype one short fish-school trial first because it extends the existing synthetic swarm narrative into real collective-motion data. Add either MIT-BIH or NASA IMS as a contrasting signal-based route. Keep the UCI datasets as lower-friction alternatives for data-analysis cohorts.
