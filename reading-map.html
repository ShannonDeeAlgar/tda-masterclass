Empirical practicals and development datasets

The following sources support empirical practicals. Their scientific and provenance details live here so that the Reader can remain mathematically focused.

Static-data practical: the grid-cell torus

Gardner and collaborators recorded simultaneous activity from modules of grid cells while rats explored different environments and slept (Gardner et al. 2022). Their CC0 data archive and reproduction materials are public.

The analysis treats selected time bins as an unordered cloud of neural population states. It therefore provides a static point-cloud investigation with a clear dynamical origin:

  1. Scientific question: what low-dimensional space appears to organise the collective activity of one grid-cell module?
  2. Observed evidence: spike trains recorded from many neurons, together with the animal’s behaviour and recording condition.
  3. Stored and processed data: smoothed firing-rate vectors, activity selection, standardisation, principal-component reduction and density-aware subsampling.
  4. Construction: a neighbourhood-based dissimilarity and persistent cohomology of the resulting neural-state cloud.
  5. Topological prediction: a torus has one dominant (H_0) class, two dominant (H_1) classes and one dominant (H_2) class.
  6. Simple baselines: PCA variance, low-dimensional visualisation and single-neuron spatial firing maps.
  7. Null comparison: independently shift each neuron’s spike train in time and repeat the complete pipeline, disrupting population coordination while retaining single-cell spike structure.
  8. Sensitivity: time-bin and smoothing width, cell selection, standardisation, activity selection, number of principal components, subsampling, distance and neighbourhood parameters.
  9. Interpretation boundary: the result supports toroidal organisation of the processed population-state cloud. The stronger continuous-attractor interpretation also depends on replication across modules, environments and sleep states and does not identify a unique neural mechanism.

The complete archive is approximately 320 MB and should not be copied into the course repository. The teaching subset must retain the source recording identifier, selected time indices, included neuron indices, all preprocessing parameters, original DOI, CC0 status and a script that regenerates it from the Figshare archive.

Static-to-dynamic bridge: motion on the grid-cell torus

The grid-cell recordings begin as time-indexed evidence even though the static analysis temporarily treats population states as an unordered cloud. The course subset therefore also retains one contiguous 20-second passage.

  1. Static question: what space is occupied by the population states?
  2. Restored dynamic object: the ordered path (tx_t) through neural activity space.
  3. Decoded route: use the two persistent (H^1) cohomology classes to obtain two circular coordinates, then analyse motion on the inferred torus.
  4. Snapshot route: turn moving windows of recent population states into clouds (W_t) and compare their persistence summaries.
  5. New choices: window length and overlap, temporal smoothing, coordinate alignment and whether within-window stationarity is plausible.
  6. Interpretation boundary: evidence that the global state space is toroidal does not determine the dynamics on it. Smoothly changing window summaries also do not establish feature identity across time.

This optional extension demonstrates that discovering a state-space topology and analysing trajectories on that space are related but distinct tasks.

Dynamic-data practical: collective-state change in a school of 300 fish

The public Fish Schooling Data Subset contains 5,000 frames of two-dimensional positions, velocities and temporary track identifiers from 300 golden shiners. The associated work identifies swarm, polarised and milling regimes using polarisation and collective rotation. A related study used the same data for unsupervised manifold-learning classification.

The course retains every fifth frame from a continuous 26.5-second passage that moves from a disordered regime through a transition and into sustained milling. It supports one scientific story across Topics 7 and 8:

  1. Scientific question: does spatial persistence register the emergence of milling organisation beyond polarisation, collective rotation, spread and detection count?
  2. Stored data: detected two-dimensional centres and velocities, source frame numbers and temporary track identifiers.
  3. Constructed representation: position clouds first; a position–velocity metric only after its relative scaling is stated.
  4. Snapshot construction: Rips filtrations at selected frames, with physical time kept distinct from filtration scale.
  5. Dynamic summary: framewise diagrams, Betti curves and a CROCKER display compared with the published order parameters.
  6. Sensitivity: spatial centring and normalisation, incomplete detection, point subsampling, filtration range and temporal sampling.
  7. Correspondence boundary: identifiers can be retired and replaced after a lost detection. They therefore support some local trajectory questions but do not provide a fixed labelled vertex set or identify homology classes through time.
  8. Interpretation boundary: a persistent annular feature may accompany a mill without establishing the behavioural mechanism that produced it.

The source JSON is 83 MB and remains in the public repository. The smaller course CSV files, exact source interval, SHA-256 digest and regeneration script are documented in the data notes.

Candidate public datasets

These are development candidates, not bundled course data. Before adopting one, retain its licence and provenance, create a documented teaching subset, and state the preprocessing and interpretation limits.

Sandstone micro-CT

The open sandstone micro-CT collection remains a specialist image and volume route. Persistent homology has a clear porous-media precedent, but the scientific payoff requires more domain background than the core course. A future teaching crop should compare connectivity across intensity or segmentation threshold with porosity and ordinary image-morphology baselines.

MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database

The PhysioNet collection could support delay-coordinate clouds from short ECG windows and comparisons through annotated rhythm transitions. Compare with heart rate, spectral power and beat annotations, and vary delay, embedding dimension and window length. Use only short, non-diagnostic excerpts and make no clinical claim.

NASA IMS bearing degradation data

The bearing dataset offers an industry route through vibration windows over a run-to-failure experiment. Compare persistence summaries with RMS vibration, kurtosis and spectral features. A course version should provide a small pre-indexed subset rather than the full raw archive.

UCI Human Activity Recognition

The smartphone activity dataset could compare accelerometer and gyroscope windows across activities or through postural transitions. Compare with covariance, spectral and classification baselines. Subject-level splitting is essential because overlapping windows must not be treated as independent observations.

UCI Air Quality

The air-quality sensor dataset could support multivariate state clouds, sensor drift and later two-parameter questions. Compare with PCA, regression residuals and seasonality. Missing values encoded as \(-200\) must be removed before computing distances, and the reuse terms should be resolved before redistributing a derived course file.
