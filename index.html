Topological Data Analysis for the Complex Systems Scientist

A conceptual route from data to persistent homology to meaning

This MasterClass is a documented route into topological data analysis for a complex systems scientist, applied mathematician or data analyst who may not have an algebraic topology background but wants more than a surface-level introduction. It may also be useful to students meeting these ideas for the first time.

The goal is conceptual fluency: to understand what a persistent homology pipeline constructs, follow the argument in a technical paper, and recognise where scientific judgement enters. Running a TDA package is relatively easy. The harder part is knowing what was constructed, which choices produced it and what the result means. The computations here make those steps visible.

The question running through the material is:

What mathematical construction addresses the scientific question, and what does that construction allow us to interpret?

No prior algebraic topology is assumed. The formal topology refresher and algebra refresher are references to consult when a definition or calculation needs unpacking.

Accompanying notebooks. Each stage has a downloadable Python notebook. The supplied example rebuilds the main object or figure from the reader, while short exercises expose the choices inside the calculation. The data input is kept separate so that the same calculation can be tested on another data set.

The persistent homology construction

By the end of the foundation material, every object and map in this construction should be clear. It is the guiding framework for everything that follows. The numbered margin notes link each part to its intuitive and formal development.

Let \(K\) be a finite simplicial complex.

1 · Build \(K\). Turn the data and modelling choices into a finite representation. Why a representation is needed and simplicial complexes in detail.

For a coefficient field \(\mathbb F\), \(H_p(K;\mathbb F)\) records its \(p\)-dimensional homology.

2 · Homology of \(K\). Calculate the homology of that representation. What homology records and chains and boundary maps.

Let

\[ f:K\to\mathbb R \]

be monotone on faces, meaning \(f(\sigma)\leq f(\tau)\) whenever \(\sigma\) is a face of \(\tau\). The sublevel complexes

\[ K_a=\{\sigma\in K:f(\sigma)\leq a\} \]

form a filtration: \(K_a\subseteq K_b\) whenever \(a\leq b\). Write \(\iota_{a,b}:K_a\hookrightarrow K_b\) for this inclusion, and use \(\mathcal K_f\) as shorthand for the entire indexed filtration and its inclusions.

3 · Filter \(K\). Repeat the construction through a nested family. Why filtrations are needed and filtrations in detail.

Applying homology gives vector spaces and induced maps

\[ V_a=H_p(K_a;\mathbb F), \qquad \phi_{a,b}=H_p(\iota_{a,b}):V_a\to V_b. \]

They satisfy

\[ \phi_{a,a}=\operatorname{id}_{V_a}, \qquad \phi_{b,c}\circ\phi_{a,b}=\phi_{a,c}. \]

The functor \(V=H_p\circ\mathcal K_f\), equivalently the family \(\{V_a,\phi_{a,b}\}_{a\leq b}\), is the degree-\(p\) persistence module. For a particular pair \(a\leq b\), the persistent homology group from \(a\) to \(b\) is

\[ H_p^{a,b}=\operatorname{im}\phi_{a,b}. \]

It contains the classes present by scale \(a\) that remain alive at scale \(b\).

4 · Persistent homology. Retain the maps that say what survives across the family. Follow classes across scale and persistence modules in detail.

For a finite one-parameter filtration over a field, the persistence module admits an interval decomposition. The same intervals are displayed as a barcode or persistence diagram:

\[ V\cong\bigoplus_{\ell}I_{[b_\ell,d_\ell)} \qquad \operatorname{Bar}_p(V),\ \operatorname{Dgm}_p(V). \]

5 · Encode the module. Record the resulting lifespans as bars or birth-death points. Barcodes and diagrams and decomposition and reduction.

The data, preprocessing, metric or relation, and the choices of \(K\) and \(f\) all precede the algebra and remain part of the scientific model.
