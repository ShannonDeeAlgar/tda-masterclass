Topological Data Analysis for Static and Dynamic Data

A conceptual route from data to persistent homology

This MasterClass develops the judgement needed to read, compute, question and adapt persistent homology pipelines for static and dynamic data.

The central outcome is conceptual fluency. By the end of the course, you should be able to follow the mathematical argument in a technical TDA paper, identify the objects and maps being used, and judge what its conclusions do and do not say about the underlying system. We construct examples and run computations because doing so makes the ideas inspectable, not because implementing every method from scratch is the final goal.

It is not organised as a software tutorial. The recurring question is:

What mathematical construction answers the scientific question, and what interpretation becomes possible or impossible because of that choice?

The guiding construction for Weeks 1–4

The expression below is likely to look like another language at first. It is included now because it is the destination of the first four weeks, not because you are expected to understand it already. Each week makes a different part of it readable.

Given a filtration of simplicial complexes

\[ K_a\subseteq K_b\qquad\text{whenever }a\leq b, \]

its \(p\)-dimensional persistent homology over a field \(\mathbb F\) is the persistence module

\[ a\longmapsto H_p(K_a;\mathbb F), \qquad (a\leq b)\longmapsto (\iota_{a,b})_*:H_p(K_a;\mathbb F)\to H_p(K_b;\mathbb F). \]

This says more than “compute holes at several scales”. It retains the homology vector space at every scale and the maps recording what each earlier class becomes later. Under suitable one-parameter finiteness or tameness conditions, the module decomposes into interval modules and can be summarised by a barcode or persistence diagram. The barcode is therefore a representation of the persistence module, not the starting definition.

How to read the formula aloud

At every parameter value \(a\), build a representation \(K_a\) of the data and compute its \(p\)-dimensional homology. Whenever \(a\leq b\), the smaller representation sits inside the larger one, and this inclusion tells us what every homology class at \(a\) becomes at \(b\). Persistent homology is the whole linked family of homology spaces and maps.

The data are not explicitly visible in the displayed definition. They enter earlier, when observations are turned into the family of representations \(K_a\). That step contains modelling choices: what is observed, how it is preprocessed, what distance or relation is used, what construction produces \(K_a\), and what parameter \(a\) controls. The formula begins after those choices have been made. Further choices remain visible in the homological dimension \(p\) and coefficient field \(\mathbb F\).

Here is how the four weeks make the expression readable:

  1. Week 1: scientific object, observations and representation. Why might connectedness, recurrence or enclosure be worth preserving, and what information is deliberately forgotten by a topological lens?
  2. Week 2: \(K_a\) and \(H_p(K_a;\mathbb F)\). What is a simplicial complex, how do boundary maps define homology, and what does the coefficient field change?
  3. Week 3: the nested family \(K_a\subseteq K_b\). How does a metric, relation or filtration function produce complexes that grow with a parameter?
  4. Week 4: the maps \((\iota_{a,b})_*\) and their interval summary. How do homology classes persist, merge or become boundaries, and when may the resulting module be represented by a barcode?

Who this course is for

Dynamical systems researchers

Use topology alongside geometric, temporal and statistical descriptions of trajectories, attractors and collective dynamics.

Honours and graduate students

Build the algebraic topology foundations needed to understand what TDA software constructs and computes.

Applied and industry participants

Evaluate whether connectedness, loops or multiscale organisation answer an operational question beyond existing baselines.

No prior algebraic topology is assumed. Topology refresher A begins with the familiar coffee-cup and doughnut intuition; Topology refresher B keeps formal open-set and continuity details available without making them prerequisites. Familiarity with basic linear algebra is helpful; the pre-Week 2 algebra survival guide supplies a concise refresher at the point where it is needed. The practical notebooks introduce the required Python code as it is used.

Software scope

Python is the course’s primary computing environment because it gives participants a coherent route through point-cloud, simplicial, cubical and persistence workflows. The notebooks use focused, inspectable code before calling specialist packages such as Ripser.py or GUDHI. The notebook setup page explains how to run them.

Julia is a planned research extension, especially when the surrounding dynamical simulation is already Julia-native or a Ripserer.jl benchmark is scientifically useful. It is not introduced merely to reproduce the same classroom barcode in a second language. A mixed-language project should pass simple objects—point arrays, distance matrices or persistence intervals—across the Python/Julia boundary rather than live complex objects. Zigzag, multiparameter and vineyard-module computation require separate method and software evaluation; ordinary persistence software does not provide them automatically.

Guiding texts and how the course uses them

Two books support the first half of the course, but they play different roles.

Edelsbrunner and Harer, Computational Topology provides the main mathematical and computational spine (Edelsbrunner and Harer 2010). We draw on its movement between geometry, topology and algorithms: connectedness in Chapter I.1; simplicial complexes, nerves, Delaunay complexes and alpha complexes in Chapter III; homology and matrix reduction in Chapter IV; one-parameter persistence and its reduction algorithm in Chapter VII; and vineyards, diagram distances and stability in Chapter VIII. It is especially useful when the course asks how a topological construction becomes something that can be represented and computed.

Postol, Algebraic Topology for Data Scientists provides the more gradual preparatory and data-facing route (Postol 2024). We use Chapter 2 for sets, metrics, topological spaces, continuity and connectedness; Chapter 3 for fields, vector spaces and the algebraic vocabulary behind homology; Chapter 4.1 for simplicial homology and induced maps; and selected parts of Chapter 5 for Rips complexes, persistence modules, barcodes, diagram distances, sublevel sets, graphs, time series, persistence images and zigzag persistence. The course does not rely on the book’s later specialised chapters on cohomology, homotopy, obstruction theory or cohomology operations.

Neither book determines the applications or software workflow. Weeks 5–8 use research papers and software documentation attached directly to the relevant week. All readings are optional routes for checking a definition, seeing a second explanation or following an idea into the literature.
