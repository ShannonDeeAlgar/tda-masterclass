Week 8 extended: vineyards and vineyard modules

Expert submodule for transport through changing filtrations

ImportantExpert-level optional submodule

This development supports specialist reading and the Discovery Project. It can be skipped without losing the Week 8 method-selection argument. The high-level idea to retain is that moving diagram points and transporting homology classes are not the same information.

Vineyard versus vineyard module

For a fixed complex \(K\) with changing filtration function \(f_t:K\to\mathbb R\), a vineyard records trajectories of persistence-diagram points. A vineyard module retains the persistence module \(V_t\) at each external time together with coherent maps between modules at different times. Schematically,

\[ \begin{array}{ccccc} V_s(a)&\longrightarrow&V_s(b)&\longrightarrow&V_s(c)\\ \downarrow&&\downarrow&&\downarrow\\ V_t(a+\varepsilon)&\longrightarrow&V_t(b+\varepsilon)&\longrightarrow&V_t(c+\varepsilon). \end{array} \]

Barcodes classify suitable individual one-parameter modules, but source and target barcodes do not classify a particular map between them. Dynamic questions may depend on those missing maps.

The Turner teaching example

Turner’s example divides a disc into four pieces (Turner 2023):

  • \(A\): open inner disc;
  • \(B\): inner boundary circle;
  • \(C\): open surrounding annulus; and
  • \(D\): outer boundary circle.

Assign

\[ f_t(A)=21-t,\qquad f_t(B)=14-t,\qquad f_t(C)=15+t,\qquad f_t(D)=t. \]

The horizontal direction is external time \(t\) and the vertical direction is filtration height. At a fixed \(t\), moving vertically reveals the sublevel-set filtration. Critical orderings change at

\[f_3(A)=f_3(C),\qquad f_7(B)=f_7(D).\]

At each time there are two \(H_1\) intervals,

\[[14-t,21-t),\qquad[t,15+t).\]

Following their endpoints through time produces two visible vines.

Why two vines need not be independent

Before the critical events, use the basis

\[[B],\qquad[D+B],\]

while later the forced basis is

\[[B],\qquad[D].\]

Over \(\mathbb F_2\),

\[[D]=[D+B]+[B].\]

The change mixes the two visible directions. After coherent simplification, an off-diagonal term remains:

\[ T=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}. \]

The off-diagonal \(1\) says that transporting the second generator necessarily acquires a contribution from the first in these bases. There is no globally coherent basis that separates the family into two independent one-dimensional problems. The vineyard module is therefore indecomposable even though the vineyard visibly contains two vines.

NoteWhat to retain if the algebra is skipped

A vineyard shows trajectories of interval endpoints. It does not determine every linear relation between the underlying homology generators. The matrix calculation is one concrete demonstration of that information gap.

Why this is delicate for moving point clouds

Smooth point motion need not produce a gently changing Rips filtration order. Many edge lengths can cross simultaneously, changing triangles and higher-dimensional simplices. Diagram stability does not imply stable feature identity:

\[ \text{diagram stability}\neq\text{reliable feature tracking}. \]

A fixed mesh carrying a smooth field, or a fixed interaction scaffold with changing weights, is often a more plausible vineyard domain than the literal Rips filtration of moving agents.
