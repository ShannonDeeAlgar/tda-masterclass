NoteOptional reading for this topic

Carlsson and de Silva develop zigzag persistence (Carlsson and Silva 2010). Xian and collaborators compare several routes for time-varying topology (Xian et al. 2022). Multiparameter and vineyard papers are linked from their expert subtopics; they are not prerequisites for the core method-selection argument.

Where this week sits. Week 7 recomputed ordinary persistence at each time or window. That is sufficient when the question is how a summary changes. Week 8 asks what additional mathematical relationships are required when the scientific question concerns continuity, correspondence, insertion, deletion or transport through physical time.

Learning goals

By the end of this topic, you should be able to:

  1. explain what repeated persistence fails to retain;
  2. distinguish tracking observations from tracking topological classes;
  3. choose among correspondence-based tracking, zigzag persistence, multiparameter persistence and vineyards by naming the required objects and maps;
  4. explain the assumptions and interpretation cost added by each choice; and
  5. recognise vineyard modules as an expert extension rather than a default method for dynamic data.

Start with the missing relationship

Suppose Week 7 produces diagrams

\[D_0,D_1,\ldots,D_T.\]

This sequence tells us how the distribution of births and deaths changes. It does not say that a point in \(D_t\) and a nearby point in \(D_{t+1}\) are the same feature. It also does not provide maps between the underlying complexes or homology spaces.

Before choosing another method, ask what relationship the scientific question requires:

Required relationship Possible route Additional assumption
Only change in summary repeated persistence frames or windows are comparable
Corresponding observations or agents track identities first, then compare local topology reliable observation-level correspondence
Complexes gain and lose simplices zigzag persistence scientifically meaningful forward and backward inclusions
Two construction parameters are jointly ordered multiparameter persistence compatible maps in both parameter directions
One fixed complex has a smoothly changing filtration vineyard fixed scaffold and controlled filtration changes
Linear transport between persistence modules matters vineyard module coherent module-level transport data

The bottom rows are not automatically better. They answer stronger questions and require stronger structure.

Route 1: track the observations before tracking topology

In particle, cell or agent data, persistent labels may already tell us which observation at time \(t\) corresponds to which observation at time \(t+1\). That does not by itself identify homology classes, but it can support a changing graph, a fixed ambient mesh or another scaffold on which maps are defensible.

When labels are unreliable, nearest-neighbour matching can confuse crossing trajectories or births and deaths of agents. A topological tracking claim is then no stronger than the correspondence model beneath it.

Dynamical systems translation. A coherent structure may move through state space while retaining its role, split into two structures, merge, or disappear. A sequence of similar Betti numbers distinguishes none of these events. Additional maps or correspondence rules are what give “the same feature” mathematical content.

Route 2: zigzag persistence for additions and deletions

Ordinary persistence assumes one growing sequence,

\[K_0\longrightarrow K_1\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow K_n.\]

If the representation gains and loses simplices, a zigzag permits arrows in both directions:

\[K_0\longrightarrow K_1\longleftarrow K_2 \longrightarrow K_3\longleftarrow\cdots.\]

A backward arrow is not an inverse operation. In \(K_i\leftarrow K_{i+1}\) it records an inclusion \(K_{i+1}\subseteq K_i\), with homology following the displayed direction. Finite zigzag modules still admit interval decompositions and zigzag barcodes (Carlsson and Silva 2010).

A transient loop

Let \(K_0\) be the path \(a-b-c\). Let \(K_1\) add \(c-a\), producing a triangular outline, and let \(K_2\) again be the path. Both paths include into the outline:

\[K_0\longrightarrow K_1\longleftarrow K_2.\]

Then \(H_1(K_0)=0\), \(H_1(K_1)\cong\mathbb F_2\) and \(H_1(K_2)=0\). The zigzag barcode contains a class supported only at the middle index. Unlike three unrelated calculations, the arrows state how the objects are connected.

Snapshots may not include directly into one another. A union zigzag is one possible bridge:

\[ K_0\to K_0\cup K_1\leftarrow K_1 \to K_1\cup K_2\leftarrow K_2. \]

The union is a modelling decision. It asserts that both snapshots embed into one meaningful common representation. That assertion must be justified for the application.

Route 3: two ordered construction parameters

Multiparameter persistence is appropriate when both axes supply compatible inclusions. The Week 7 expert subtopic develops a scale-by-codensity example.

Physical time can be a second ordered parameter for cumulative data, such as all observations acquired up to \(t\). Independent frames generally do not form a bifiltration merely because their timestamps are ordered.

NoteExpert-level detail: safe to skip

General multiparameter modules lack the complete interval-barcode classification available in one parameter. Understanding the two families of compatible maps is sufficient here; representation theory and specialised invariants are optional.

Route 4: vineyards on a fixed changing scaffold

Suppose one fixed complex \(K\) carries a filtration function \(f_t:K\to\mathbb R\) that changes continuously with external time \(t\). At each time, the sublevel filtration produces a diagram \(D_t\). A vineyard follows the birth-death points as curves, called vines, through birth-death-time space (Cohen-Steiner et al. 2006).

The high-level picture is a landscape whose height function changes. Ordinary persistence floods one fixed landscape. A vineyard follows persistence while the landscape itself deforms.

The assumptions matter. The simplices remain fixed and only their filtration values vary in a controlled way. A moving point-cloud Rips filtration may violate this picture because many distance orderings and simplices can change globally.

A vineyard is more than a stack of unrelated diagrams, but it is not automatically a complete account of linear transport between modules.

For the detailed critical-value construction, transport matrix and indecomposability argument, continue to the expert vineyard submodule.

Choosing a fixed scaffold

For dynamic applications, two plausible scaffolds are:

  1. a fixed interaction graph or clique complex whose weights change through time; or
  2. a fixed ambient grid or mesh carrying a changing density, activity or potential field.

These choices change the object being interpreted. The resulting topology belongs to the interaction scaffold or ambient field, not directly to the raw moving points.

What can be skipped

For the general MasterClass audience, retain the method-selection logic and the transient zigzag example. The following are expert-level developments:

  • multiparameter classification and algorithms;
  • vineyard update algorithms;
  • coherent inter-module transport maps;
  • vineyard modules and indecomposability; and
  • paper-specific matrix decompositions.

Skipping those details does not interrupt the main course argument.

Planned data example

NoteA real dynamic dataset is still needed

This topic currently uses transparent mathematical and synthetic examples. A later data module should compare repeated persistence with one justified non-snapshot method on a public time-varying dataset. It must state the correspondence or scaffold assumptions, use an ordinary dynamic baseline, and show when the added structure changes the conclusion.

Interpretation boundary

No advanced method creates physical feature identity from timestamps alone. Zigzags, multiparameter modules and vineyards formalise different kinds of relationships. The scientific conclusion is only as defensible as the maps, correspondences and scaffolds used to construct them.
