Dynamic data: relationships through change

Practical notebook. Compute snapshot persistence for a changing point cloud and inspect Betti summaries across time and scale. Download the notebook.

Dynamic data add a second question to the persistent homology pipeline. We must decide not only what structure to calculate within each observation, but also what relationship, if any, connects observations through change.

A sequence of snapshot diagrams can show that topological summaries change. It does not say that a point in \(D_t\) and a nearby point in \(D_{t+1}\) are the same feature.

Going beyond snapshots requires additional structure. Different methods encode different kinds of relationship:

Scientific need Mathematical route Structure that must be justified
compare changing summaries snapshot persistence comparable frames or windows
follow known agents or cells track observations first reliable identities or correspondence
represent additions and removals zigzag persistence inclusions that can point forwards or backwards
retain two ordered construction choices multiparameter persistence compatible maps in both parameter directions
follow persistence pairings along controlled change vineyard a specified path of filtrations

These methods are not ranked from basic to superior. Each makes a stronger claim because it assumes a stronger relationship.

Identity needs evidence. Timestamps order observations but do not create feature identity. Any claim that a component, loop or cavity is the same one later must come from tracking, inclusions or another stated correspondence.

Two axes are not enough. A heat map over physical time and filtration scale is a useful Betti surface. It becomes a two-parameter persistence module only when both directions carry compatible maps.

Observation identity comes first when it is available

Tracked particles, cells or agents can support a changing graph or a fixed ambient scaffold. Their labels do not automatically identify homology classes, but they provide information absent from unlabelled snapshots.

When trajectories cross, observations enter or leave, or detections are missing, nearest-neighbour matching can invent continuity. Any later claim about a persistent topological feature is then conditional on that tracking model.

Zigzag persistence: allow structure to enter and leave

Ordinary persistence follows nested growth. A zigzag permits inclusions in both displayed directions:

\[ K_0\longrightarrow K_1\longleftarrow K_2 \longrightarrow K_3\longleftarrow\cdots. \]

This is useful when complexes gain and lose simplices but can still be related through meaningful inclusions. Finite zigzag modules retain an interval decomposition, so their lifetimes can still be represented by a barcode (Carlsson and Silva 2010).

For example, a path may gain one closing edge and later lose it. The loop exists only in the middle complex. The arrows distinguish this connected event from three unrelated Betti-number calculations.

Multiparameter persistence: retain two ordered choices

Scale and density, distance and interaction order, or threshold and spatial resolution may both define inclusions. A multiparameter module retains those directions rather than imposing an artificial total ordering. General multiparameter modules do not admit the complete interval-barcode classification available in one parameter (Carlsson and Zomorodian 2009). Rank invariants, slices and dimension functions therefore summarise selected aspects rather than completely classifying the object.

Independent time snapshots do not generally form this structure. Time can be an ordered parameter when observations accumulate or another construction supplies genuine inclusions.

Vineyards: follow pairings along a path of filtrations

Suppose a fixed complex \(K\) carries a filtration function \(f_t\) that changes with external time \(t\). Each \(f_t\) produces one persistence diagram. A vineyard follows its persistence points along the specified path of filtrations, producing curves called vines (Cohen-Steiner et al. 2006).

External time selects the current filtration. Birth and death coordinates remain values of the filtration parameter, not timestamps.

Why a vineyard is more than a stack. The path of changing filtration values determines a sequence of ordering events in the filtered complex. The algorithm updates persistence pairings through those events rather than joining whichever plotted points happen to be nearest.

A fixed mesh carrying a changing field or a fixed interaction complex with changing weights is a natural vineyard setting. A Rips complex of freely moving points is more delicate because many distances and simplices can exchange order.

You are ready to continue when you can select a route from the relationship required by the scientific question, explain why diagram proximity is not feature identity, distinguish a Betti surface from a multiparameter module and state what fixed structure makes a vineyard plausible.
