Applied glossary

Pure-mathematical terms translated without losing their conditions

How to use this glossary

The middle column gives an applied intuition, not a replacement definition. The final column records the boundary of that intuition. When a term is used in a scientific claim, return to the precise meaning and name the constructed object.

Objects and representations

Mathematical term Precise meaning Applied or intuitive translation Interpretation caution
Topological space A set equipped with open sets satisfying the topology axioms. A specification of which points count as locally near one another, without requiring numerical distance. “Topology” is not a generic synonym for shape or network layout.
Homeomorphism A bijection whose forward and inverse maps are continuous. A reversible deformation that preserves topological structure. The rubber-sheet metaphor is only an intuition, and finite samples are not automatically homeomorphic to latent continuous spaces.
Homotopy equivalence Maps in both directions whose composites are homotopic to the corresponding identity maps. Two spaces retain the same broad continuous shape after contractible detail is ignored. It is weaker than homeomorphism and does not preserve metric geometry.
Topological invariant A quantity or algebraic object unchanged under homeomorphism. A shape descriptor designed to ignore coordinates and reversible deformation. In data analysis it is computed on a constructed representation, not directly on an unknown physical system.
Constructed representation A mathematical object built from observations and modelling choices. The point cloud, graph, image complex or embedding through which the system is analysed. It can amplify, erase or invent apparent organisation relative to the scientific object.
Metric A distance function satisfying non-negativity, identity, symmetry and the triangle inequality. The rule that decides which observations are similar enough to interact first. A convenient metric may be scientifically inappropriate.
Point cloud A finite subset of a metric space. A collection of sampled states or observations represented as points. It is a representation of the system, not automatically the system or attractor itself.
Geometric descriptor A quantity or representation measuring a specified metric or coordinate property of a geometric object. One deliberately chosen answer to “what makes these shapes look different?”, such as spacing, area, direction or curvature. No short list of descriptors is a complete definition of shape; inspect what each retains and discards.
Convex hull The smallest convex set containing a specified set of points. A tight elastic band placed around the outermost observations. It records the outer envelope but cannot detect internal gaps, density or concavity.
Anisotropy Dependence of measured properties on direction. Evidence that a cloud stretches or organises more strongly along some directions than others. A single anisotropy ratio can miss curvature, multiple branches and local changes in direction.
Geometric simplex The convex hull of affinely independent vertices. A filled-in building block: point, segment, triangle, tetrahedron. A drawn triangle and three edges do not specify whether the face is filled.
Abstract simplex A finite set of vertices. A recorded multiway relation among the listed vertices. It need not have physical extent or a unique embedding.
Simplicial complex A collection of simplices closed under taking faces. A consistent catalogue of relations at every included order. Its 1-skeleton does not determine its higher-dimensional simplices.
Geometric realisation \(|K|\) A topological space obtained by realising and gluing the simplices of \(K\). A spatial version of the abstract incidence data. The realisation is not necessarily the observed physical object.
1-skeleton All vertices and edges of a complex. The network visible after discarding filled triangles and higher relations. Homology of the graph may differ from homology of the full complex.
Clique complex The complex containing a simplex for every clique of a graph. Treat every mutually pairwise-connected group as one higher-order unit. Clique filling is a modelling assumption about higher-order coherence.
Graph cycle rank For a finite undirected graph, \(E-V+C\), the dimension of its cycle space over a field. The number of independent network loops before any higher-dimensional filling is added. It is not generally \(H_1\) of the clique complex because triangles and higher simplices can fill graph cycles.

Homology as linear algebra

Mathematical term Precise meaning Applied or intuitive translation Interpretation caution
Basis A linearly independent spanning set giving every vector unique coordinates. The agreed list of elementary pieces used to record a combination. Coordinates depend on the selected basis even when the vector does not.
Span All linear combinations of a specified set of vectors. Everything that can be assembled from the available building blocks. A long generating list may contain redundant vectors.
Kernel For \(T:V\to W\), the subspace \(\{v:T(v)=0\}\). Input combinations whose effects cancel completely under the map. Being in a kernel is relative to a particular map.
Image For \(T:V\to W\), the subspace \(\{T(v):v\in V\}\). Output patterns that the available inputs can actually produce. The image need not equal the full codomain.
Rank The dimension of the image of a linear map. The number of independent output directions produced by the map. Matrix rank is basis-independent, though the matrix entries are not.
Nullity The dimension of the kernel of a linear map. The number of independent input directions erased by the map. A large nullity has meaning only after the domain and map are named.
Quotient vector space \(V/W\) Equivalence classes under \(v\sim v'\) when \(v-v'\in W\). Treat differences lying in \(W\) as irrelevant or already accounted for. The slash is not numerical division, and a class has many representatives.
Equivalence class \([v]\) All elements equivalent to \(v\) under a specified relation. Every representation treated as the same after the agreed differences are ignored. A displayed representative is not canonical unless additional structure selects it.
Coefficient field The field used for chain coefficients, here usually \(\mathbb F_2\). The arithmetic system used to combine and cancel building blocks. Features can depend on coefficients, especially in the presence of torsion.
Chain space \(C_p\) The vector space freely generated by the \(p\)-simplices. All formal selections or combinations of \(p\)-dimensional pieces. A chain is algebraic bookkeeping, not necessarily one connected geometric object.
Boundary map \(\partial_p\) The linear map sending each \(p\)-simplex to the signed sum of its faces. A rule extracting the exposed lower-dimensional boundary of a collection of pieces. Over \(\mathbb F_2\) signs vanish; over other coefficients orientation matters.
Cycle \(Z_p\) A chain in \(\ker\partial_p\). A closed algebraic pattern with no remaining boundary. A cycle need not represent a nonzero hole; it may itself be a boundary.
Boundary \(B_p\) A chain in \(\operatorname{im}\partial_{p+1}\). A closed pattern already filled by something one dimension higher. “Boundary” here is algebraic, not merely the visible edge of a plotted region.
Homology \(H_p\) The quotient \(Z_p/B_p\). Closed patterns counted only after filled or equivalent versions are identified. A class is an equivalence class of cycles, not a privileged loop.
Betti number \(\beta_p\) The dimension of \(H_p\) over the chosen field. The number of independent \(p\)-dimensional topological degrees of freedom. It is a count at one object or scale and contains no feature identity.
Euler characteristic \(\chi\) The alternating sum of simplex counts, equivalently Betti numbers for a finite complex. A compact accounting checksum for the complex. Different homology can share the same Euler characteristic.

Filtrations and persistence

Mathematical term Precise meaning Applied or intuitive translation Interpretation caution
Filtration A nested family \(K_a\subseteq K_b\) for \(a\leq b\). A controlled sweep through a modelling threshold. The parameter is not automatically physical time.
Filtration function A face-monotone value assigned to every simplex. The rule deciding when each relation becomes available. Birth inherits meaning from this rule, not from topology alone.
Čech complex The nerve of equal-radius balls centred on a point cloud. Record groups whose neighbourhoods share a genuinely common point. Joint intersection is stronger than pairwise intersection.
Vietoris–Rips complex The clique complex on pairs below a distance threshold. Infer higher-order groups from pairwise proximity. Check whether the parameter denotes radius or pairwise-distance threshold.
Inclusion-induced map The map \(H_p(K_a)\to H_p(K_b)\) induced by \(K_a\hookrightarrow K_b\). The rule describing what an earlier feature becomes after more simplices are added. The map need not be injective; an old class can map to zero.
Functor A rule sending objects to objects and maps to maps while preserving identities and composition. A consistent translation that retains both the states and how one state maps to another. The word adds no new empirical information; it records a structural compatibility.
Persistence module Vector spaces \(H_p(K_a)\) together with all compatible maps across scale. Homology with its cross-scale memory retained. A sequence of Betti numbers is not a persistence module.
Birth The first parameter at which a class appears in the module. The threshold where a new independent organisation becomes detectable. It is construction-dependent and may arise from sampling or ties.
Death The parameter where a class merges into an older class or becomes a boundary. The threshold where that independent organisation is no longer distinguishable. The underlying chain may still exist after its class dies.
Persistence Death minus birth for a finite interval. The range of thresholds over which a class remains distinguishable. Long persistence is not automatically scientific importance.
Interval module A module equal to the field on one interval and zero elsewhere. One idealised feature that is alive over one parameter range. The full module is usually a direct sum of many intervals.
Barcode The multiset of intervals in an interval decomposition. A timeline-like display of feature lifetimes across scale. Classification by barcodes needs suitable one-parameter finiteness or tameness assumptions.
Persistence diagram Birth-death points encoding the same interval data. A scatter plot of when features appear and disappear. Distance from the diagonal measures persistence, not truth or usefulness.
Representative cycle A cycle chosen from a homology class. One concrete loop or void boundary used to localise an abstract feature. The choice is generally not unique, so its precise location should not be treated as canonical.
Essential class A class with no finite death in the available filtration. Structure that remains after the sweep reaches its endpoint. It may be genuinely global or merely censored by a truncated parameter range.
Stability A theorem bounding diagram change by an appropriate change in input or filtration. Small permitted perturbations cannot arbitrarily move the persistence output. Stability does not imply interpretability, significance or predictive value.

Dynamic data

Mathematical term Precise meaning Applied or intuitive translation Interpretation caution
Repeated persistence Separate static persistence computations at successive times or windows. A movie of barcode summaries. Similar-looking bars are not automatically the same tracked feature.
Vineyard Trajectories of persistence-diagram points under a changing function. Feature summaries traced continuously as data evolve. Matching diagram points is additional structure beyond repeated diagrams.
Zigzag persistence Persistence for diagrams whose maps may point forward or backward. Track structure when observations or complexes can be added and removed. It solves a different problem from an ordinary increasing filtration.
Multiparameter persistence A module indexed by a partial order with two or more parameters. Retain several genuine modelling axes without collapsing them prematurely. There is generally no complete barcode classification analogous to the one-parameter case.
Simplex tree A data structure storing simplices, faces and filtration values in a prefix tree. A computational index for querying and updating a filtered complex. It is a representation in software, not a new topological object.
Cubical complex A complex built from vertices, edges, pixels, voxels and higher-dimensional cubes. A topology-ready representation of gridded images, fields or volumes. Cell-value conventions and sublevel direction determine birth meaning.
Lower-star filtration A filtration assigning each simplex the maximum value on its vertices. Activate a relation when its highest-valued required vertex becomes available. Negating values changes a low-feature question into a high-feature question.
Bottleneck distance The minimum possible maximum matching cost between diagrams, allowing diagonal matches. The size of the single worst discrepancy after optimal matching. It can ignore the accumulated effect of many smaller changes.
Wasserstein distance An optimal matching cost that aggregates powered pointwise costs. Total diagram discrepancy rather than only the worst match. Its value depends on the order, ground metric and treatment of diagonal matches.
Betti curve The function counting intervals alive at each filtration value. A multiscale feature-count profile. It discards interval identity and can map different diagrams to the same curve.
Persistence image A weighted, smoothed rasterisation of diagram points. A fixed-length feature array for statistics or machine learning. Resolution, smoothing and weighting are modelling parameters.
Persistence entropy Shannon entropy of normalised interval lengths, usually for finite intervals. A scalar describing how evenly persistence is distributed across features. It discards birth locations and feature identity, and conventions for essential or zero-length intervals matter.
Mapper A graph or simplicial complex built from overlapping bins of a filter function and clusters within their inverse images. A compressed atlas of how observations organise along a chosen lens. Its topology depends on the filter, cover and clustering, and it is not a persistence diagram.
Null model A probability model representing structure expected without the target mechanism. The comparison world against which “unusual” is defined. An easy-to-simulate null may preserve the wrong properties.
Surrogate data Synthetic or transformed observations generated under a null while preserving selected features. A controlled negative comparison passed through the same pipeline. Validity depends on what is preserved, destroyed and exchangeable.
CROCKER-style plot A display of Betti number or rank across physical time and filtration scale. A heat map of how multiscale topological counts evolve. It summarises repeated diagrams and does not track class identity.
SW1PerS A sliding-window persistent-homology method for quantifying periodicity in signals. Turn repeated signal phases into a loop-like point cloud and measure its persistence. Lag, window dimension, sampling and normalisation shape the result; it is not a generic feature-tracking method.
Chromatic persistence Persistence constructions that retain labels or colours on points. Ask how different populations mix, surround or contribute to topological features. It changes the construction and requires meaningful labels; ordinary persistence cannot recover labels after they are discarded.
Physical time \(t\) The external index describing when a system is observed. Clock time, trial progress or window position. It must not be confused with filtration scale.
Filtration scale \(\varepsilon\) The ordered threshold within one persistence calculation. The resolution or relation-strength dial used to construct topology. It is not necessarily a physical unit unless the construction supports that interpretation.
Feature tracking An explicit correspondence assigning identity to features across an external index. Saying which evolving feature is the continuation of which earlier feature. Nearest diagram points or similar lifetimes do not automatically establish identity.
Interleaving Compatible shifted maps between persistence modules. A controlled way to compare modules while allowing parameter tolerance. A small interleaving distance does not uniquely match generators.
Vineyard module A time-indexed family of persistence modules with coherent transport maps. Vines plus the algebra describing how feature directions mix through time. It is richer than a vineyard and requires additional structure to estimate.

Maintenance rule

When a new mathematical term enters the course, add it here with:

  1. a precise definition at the level used in the course;
  2. an applied translation tied to a data or scientific decision; and
  3. one sentence stating where that translation can mislead.
