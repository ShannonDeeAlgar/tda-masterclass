Applied glossary

Pure-mathematical terms translated without losing their conditions

Objects and representations

Mathematical term Precise meaning Applied or intuitive translation Interpretation caution
Set A collection of elements. The collection of items currently being treated as one mathematical object. A set alone carries no distance, neighbourhood or continuity structure.
Point An element of a set when that set is being treated as a space. One possible location, state or observation in the represented space. “Point” describes its role in the space, not necessarily a literal spatial dot.
Open set A member of the topology; in a metric space, every included point has a sufficiently small ball contained in the set. A region that leaves local room around each of its points. Openness belongs to the chosen topology, not to a subset in isolation.
Topology A collection of subsets containing the empty set and whole space and closed under arbitrary unions and finite intersections. The declared neighbourhood structure that makes continuity meaningful. It is not a generic synonym for shape.
Topological space A set equipped with open sets satisfying the topology axioms. A specification of which points count as locally near one another, without requiring numerical distance. “Topology” is not a generic synonym for shape or network layout.
Continuous map A map whose preimage of every open set is open. A transformation that does not introduce abrupt topological breaks. Continuity depends on the topologies on both domain and codomain.
Connected space A space that cannot be separated into two disjoint non-empty open subsets. One piece at the level of continuous organisation. Connected does not always mean path-connected.
Homeomorphism A bijection whose forward and inverse maps are continuous. A reversible deformation that preserves topological structure. The rubber sheet metaphor concerns continuous spaces. A bare finite sample is discrete, so a data analysis first needs a stated finite representation.
Homotopy equivalence Maps in both directions whose composites are homotopic to the corresponding identity maps. Two spaces retain the same broad continuous shape after contractible detail is ignored. It is weaker than homeomorphism and does not preserve metric geometry.
Homotopy A continuous deformation from one map to another. A continuously varying family of transformations. Homotopy relates maps; homotopy equivalence of spaces requires maps in both directions with suitable composites.
Contractible space A space homotopy equivalent to a point. A space whose topological structure can be continuously collapsed away. Contractible does not mean geometrically small.
Deformation retract A continuous deformation of a space onto a subspace that keeps every point of the subspace fixed. Collapse dispensable material while leaving a chosen structural core unmoved. The retract must remain inside the original space throughout the deformation.
Path-connected Every pair of points can be joined by a continuous path lying in the space. Any location can be reached continuously from any other without leaving the object. Path-connectedness implies connectedness, but the converse can fail for unusual spaces.
Topological invariant A quantity or algebraic object unchanged under homeomorphism. A shape descriptor designed to ignore coordinates and reversible deformation. In data analysis it is computed on a constructed representation, not directly on an unknown physical system.
Constructed representation A mathematical object built from observations and modelling choices. The point cloud, graph, image complex or embedding through which the system is analysed. It can amplify, erase or invent apparent organisation relative to the scientific object.
Metric A distance function satisfying non-negativity, identity, symmetry and the triangle inequality. The rule that decides which observations are similar enough to interact first. A convenient metric may be scientifically inappropriate.
Point cloud A finite subset of a metric space. A collection of sampled states or observations represented as points. It is a representation of the system, not automatically the system or attractor itself.
Geometric descriptor A quantity or representation measuring a specified metric or coordinate property of a geometric object. One deliberately chosen answer to “what makes these shapes look different?”, such as spacing, area, direction or curvature. No short list of descriptors is a complete definition of shape; inspect what each retains and discards.
Convex hull The smallest convex set containing a specified set of points. A tight elastic band placed around the outermost observations. It records the outer envelope but cannot detect internal gaps, density or concavity.
Anisotropy Dependence of measured properties on direction. Evidence that a cloud stretches or organises more strongly along some directions than others. A single anisotropy ratio can miss curvature, multiple branches and local changes in direction.
Geometric simplex The convex hull of affinely independent vertices. A filled-in building block: point, segment, triangle, tetrahedron. A drawn triangle and three edges do not specify whether the face is filled.
Abstract simplex A finite set of vertices. A recorded multiway relation among the listed vertices. It need not have physical extent or a unique embedding.
Vertex A 0-simplex and basic element from which higher simplices are formed. One observation, state, agent or other basic item in the representation. A plotted location is a geometric placement of the vertex, not the abstract vertex itself.
Edge A 1-simplex containing two vertices. A declaration that two represented items count as related. The scientific meaning of the relation comes from the construction.
Filled triangle A 2-simplex containing three vertices and all of its faces. A coherent three-way relation or filled patch rather than an empty three-edge loop. Three pairwise edges do not by themselves force a filled triangle unless the construction says so.
Simplex dimension A \(p\)-simplex has \(p+1\) vertices; vertices, edges and filled triangles are 0-, 1- and 2-simplices. The dimension names the order of the filled building block. A 2-simplex is filled; a triangular outline contains only 0- and 1-simplices.
Simplicial complex A collection of simplices closed under taking faces. A consistent catalogue of relations at every included order. Its 1-skeleton does not determine its higher-dimensional simplices.
Downward closure Every non-empty face of an included simplex must also belong to the complex. A group relation brings all of its required lower-order pieces with it. This condition distinguishes a simplicial complex from a general hypergraph.
Geometric realisation \(|K|\) A topological space obtained by realising and gluing the simplices of \(K\). A spatial version of the abstract incidence data. The realisation is not necessarily the observed physical object.
1-skeleton All vertices and edges of a complex. The network visible after discarding filled triangles and higher relations. Homology of the graph may differ from homology of the full complex.
Subcomplex A downward-closed subset of the simplices of a complex. A smaller valid complex contained within a larger one. An arbitrary selection of simplices need not be a subcomplex.
Clique complex The complex containing a simplex for every clique of a graph. Treat every mutually pairwise-connected group as one higher-order unit. Clique filling is a modelling assumption about higher-order coherence.
Cubical complex A complex built from vertices, edges, pixels, voxels and higher-dimensional cubes. A topology-ready representation of gridded images, fields or volumes. Cell-value conventions and filtration direction determine what birth means.
Triangulation A simplicial complex whose geometric realisation represents a given topological space. A finite assembly of simplices used to model the continuous object. Different triangulations can represent the same space.
Graph cycle rank For a finite undirected graph, \(E-V+C\), the dimension of its cycle space over a field. The number of independent network loops before any higher-dimensional filling is added. It is not generally \(H_1\) of the clique complex because triangles and higher simplices can fill graph cycles.

Homology as linear algebra

Mathematical term Precise meaning Applied or intuitive translation Interpretation caution
Basis A linearly independent spanning set giving every vector unique coordinates. The agreed list of elementary pieces used to record a combination. Coordinates depend on the selected basis even when the vector does not.
Span All linear combinations of a specified set of vectors. Everything that can be assembled from the available building blocks. A long generating list may contain redundant vectors.
Kernel For \(T:V\to W\), the subspace \(\{v:T(v)=0\}\). Input combinations whose effects cancel completely under the map. Being in a kernel is relative to a particular map.
Image For \(T:V\to W\), the subspace \(\{T(v):v\in V\}\). Output patterns that the available inputs can actually produce. The image need not equal the full codomain.
Rank The dimension of the image of a linear map. The number of independent output directions produced by the map. Matrix rank is basis-independent, though the matrix entries are not.
Nullity The dimension of the kernel of a linear map. The number of independent input directions erased by the map. A large nullity has meaning only after the domain and map are named.
Rank-nullity theorem For finite-dimensional \(V\), \(\dim V=\operatorname{rank}T+\operatorname{nullity}T\). Every independent input direction is either retained in the image or lost to the kernel. The split is relative to the stated linear map.
Quotient vector space \(V/W\) Equivalence classes under \(v\sim v'\) when \(v-v'\in W\). Treat differences lying in \(W\) as irrelevant or already accounted for. The slash is not numerical division, and a class has many representatives.
Equivalence class \([v]\) All elements equivalent to \(v\) under a specified relation. Every representation treated as the same after the agreed differences are ignored. A displayed representative is not canonical unless additional structure selects it.
Coefficient field The field used for chain coefficients, here usually \(\mathbb F_2\). The arithmetic system used to combine and cancel building blocks. Features can depend on coefficients, especially in the presence of torsion.
Chain space \(C_p\) The vector space freely generated by the \(p\)-simplices. All formal selections or combinations of \(p\)-dimensional pieces. A chain is algebraic bookkeeping, not necessarily one connected geometric object.
Chain complex Vector spaces \(C_p\) linked by boundary maps with \(\partial_{p-1}\partial_p=0\). The simplicial complex recorded dimension by dimension, together with how each layer meets the one below. A chain complex is algebraic data derived from \(K\); it is not another geometric complex.
Boundary map \(\partial_p\) The linear map sending each \(p\)-simplex to the signed sum of its faces. A rule extracting the exposed lower-dimensional boundary of a collection of pieces. Over \(\mathbb F_2\) signs vanish; over other coefficients orientation matters.
Cycle \(Z_p\) A chain in \(\ker\partial_p\). A closed algebraic pattern with no remaining boundary. A cycle need not represent a nonzero hole; it may itself be a boundary.
Boundary \(B_p\) A chain in \(\operatorname{im}\partial_{p+1}\). A closed pattern already filled by something one dimension higher. “Boundary” here is algebraic, not merely the visible edge of a plotted region.
Homology \(H_p\) An algebraic invariant of a space; for a simplicial complex it is calculated as the quotient \(Z_p/B_p\). It records connected pieces and independent unfilled cycles in each dimension. It is coarser than homotopy and depends on the represented space and coefficient system.
Homology class An equivalence class \([z]=z+B_p\) of cycles that differ by boundaries. One topological feature represented by any of several cycles with the same unfilled content. A class is not a unique geometric loop; representative cycles can differ by an available boundary.
Betti number \(\beta_p\) The dimension of \(H_p\) over the chosen field. The number of independent \(p\)-dimensional topological degrees of freedom. It is a count at one object or scale and contains no feature identity.
Euler characteristic \(\chi\) The alternating sum of simplex counts, equivalently Betti numbers for a finite complex. A compact accounting checksum for the complex. Different homology can share the same Euler characteristic.

Filtrations and persistence

Mathematical term Precise meaning Applied or intuitive translation Interpretation caution
Filtration A nested family \(K_a\subseteq K_b\) for \(a\leq b\). A controlled sweep through a modelling threshold. The parameter is not automatically physical time.
Filtration function A face-monotone value assigned to every simplex. The rule deciding when each relation becomes available. Birth inherits meaning from this rule, not from topology alone.
Čech complex The nerve of equal-radius balls centred on a point cloud. Record groups whose neighbourhoods share a genuinely common point. Joint intersection is stronger than pairwise intersection.
Vietoris–Rips complex The clique complex on pairs below a distance threshold. Infer higher-order groups from pairwise proximity. Check whether the parameter denotes radius or pairwise-distance threshold.
Inclusion-induced map The map \(H_p(K_a)\to H_p(K_b)\) induced by \(K_a\hookrightarrow K_b\). The rule describing what an earlier feature becomes after more simplices are added. The map need not be injective; an old class can map to zero.
Inclusion map For \(K_a\subseteq K_b\), the map that sends each simplex of \(K_a\) to the same simplex in \(K_b\). The bookkeeping link that says the earlier complex sits inside the later one. It is a map of complexes, not yet the induced map between their homology spaces.
Functor A rule sending objects to objects and maps to maps while preserving identities and composition. A consistent translation that retains both the states and how one state maps to another. The word adds no new empirical information; it records a structural compatibility.
Persistence module Vector spaces \(H_p(K_a)\) together with all compatible maps across scale. Homology with its cross-scale memory retained. A sequence of Betti numbers is not a persistence module.
Topological feature A homology class considered as it appears and persists through a filtration. A connected component, loop or cavity being followed across scale. It is an algebraic class, not necessarily one unique visible shape or representative cycle.
Birth The first parameter at which a class appears in the module. The threshold where a new independent organisation becomes detectable. It is construction-dependent and may arise from sampling or ties.
Death The parameter where a class merges into an older class or becomes a boundary. The threshold where that independent organisation is no longer distinguishable. The underlying chain may still exist after its class dies.
Persistence Death minus birth for a finite interval. The range of thresholds over which a class remains distinguishable. Long persistence is not automatically scientific importance.
Interval module A module equal to the field on one interval and zero elsewhere. One idealised feature that is alive over one parameter range. The full module is usually a direct sum of many intervals.
Barcode The multiset of intervals in an interval decomposition. A timeline-like display of feature lifetimes across scale. Classification by barcodes needs suitable one-parameter finiteness or tameness assumptions.
Persistence diagram Birth-death points encoding the same interval data. A scatter plot of when features appear and disappear. Distance from the diagonal measures persistence, not truth or usefulness.
Representative cycle A cycle chosen from a homology class. One concrete loop or void boundary used to localise an abstract feature. The choice is generally not unique, so its precise location should not be treated as canonical.
Essential class A class with no finite death in the available filtration. Structure that remains after the sweep reaches its endpoint. It may be genuinely global or merely censored by a truncated parameter range.
Stability A theorem bounding diagram change by an appropriate change in input or filtration. Small permitted perturbations cannot arbitrarily move the persistence output. Stability does not imply interpretability, significance or predictive value.

Dynamic data

Mathematical term Precise meaning Applied or intuitive translation Interpretation caution
Snapshot persistence Separate static persistence computations at successive times or windows. A movie of barcode summaries. Similar-looking bars are not automatically the same tracked feature.
Dynamic point cloud A family of finite point sets \(X_t\) indexed by physical time or another external variable. A cloud whose observed locations, membership or both change from frame to frame. The indexing alone supplies no correspondence between points or topological classes.
Betti surface A function or display of Betti number across external time and filtration scale. A two-dimensional view of how feature counts vary through time and resolution. It counts classes but does not track their identities.
Vineyard proxy A visual stack of independently computed persistence diagrams. A preliminary view of how diagram points appear to move. Without justified correspondence it is not a vineyard.
Vineyard Trajectories of persistence-diagram points under a changing function. Feature summaries traced continuously as data evolve. Matching diagram points is additional structure beyond repeated diagrams.
Zigzag persistence Persistence for diagrams whose maps may point forward or backward. Track structure when observations or complexes can be added and removed. It solves a different problem from an ordinary increasing filtration.
Multiparameter persistence A module indexed by a partial order with two or more parameters. Retain several genuine modelling axes without collapsing them prematurely. There is generally no complete barcode classification analogous to the one-parameter case.
Lower-star filtration A filtration assigning each simplex the maximum value on its vertices. Activate a relation when its highest-valued required vertex becomes available. Negating values changes a low-feature question into a high-feature question.
Bottleneck distance The minimum possible maximum matching cost between diagrams, allowing diagonal matches. The size of the single worst discrepancy after optimal matching. It can ignore the accumulated effect of many smaller changes.
Wasserstein distance An optimal matching cost that aggregates powered pointwise costs. Total diagram discrepancy rather than only the worst match. Its value depends on the order, ground metric and treatment of diagonal matches.
Betti curve The function counting intervals alive at each filtration value. A multiscale feature-count profile. It discards interval identity and can map different diagrams to the same curve.
Persistence landscape Ordered functions obtained from the triangular functions associated with persistence intervals. A functional representation of interval prominence across scale. Truncating levels or discretising the scale axis loses information.
Persistence image A weighted, smoothed rasterisation of diagram points. A fixed-length feature array for statistics or machine learning. Resolution, smoothing and weighting are modelling parameters.
Persistence entropy Shannon entropy of normalised interval lengths, usually for finite intervals. A scalar describing how evenly persistence is distributed across features. It discards birth locations and feature identity, and conventions for essential or zero-length intervals matter.
Maximum persistence The largest finite lifespan \(\max_i(d_i-b_i)\) in a selected diagram. The lifetime of the single longest-lived class. It ignores every other class and depends on filtration units.
Total persistence An aggregate such as \(\sum_i(d_i-b_i)^q\) over selected finite intervals. The accumulated lifetime carried by the diagram. Its value depends on the exponent, diagram size and treatment of essential intervals.
Null model A probability model representing structure expected without the target mechanism. The comparison world against which “unusual” is defined. An easy-to-simulate null may preserve the wrong properties.
Surrogate data Synthetic or transformed observations generated under a null while preserving selected features. A controlled negative comparison passed through the same pipeline. Validity depends on what is preserved, destroyed and exchangeable.
CROCKER-style plot A display of Betti number or rank across physical time and filtration scale. A heat map of how multiscale topological counts evolve. It summarises repeated diagrams and does not track class identity.
SW1PerS A sliding-window persistent homology method for quantifying periodicity in signals. Turn repeated signal phases into a loop-like point cloud and measure its persistence. Lag, window dimension, sampling and normalisation shape the result; it is not a generic feature-tracking method.
Physical time \(t\) The external index describing when a system is observed. Clock time, trial progress or window position. It must not be confused with filtration scale.
Filtration scale \(\varepsilon\) The ordered threshold within one persistence calculation. The resolution or relation-strength dial used to construct topology. It is not necessarily a physical unit unless the construction supports that interpretation.
Feature tracking An explicit correspondence assigning identity to features across an external index. Saying which evolving feature is the continuation of which earlier feature. Nearest diagram points or similar lifetimes do not automatically establish identity.
Vineyard module A time-indexed family of persistence modules with coherent transport maps. Vines plus the algebra describing how feature directions mix through time. It is richer than a vineyard and requires additional structure to estimate.

Maintenance rule

Bold type in the main Reader prose is reserved for a reusable technical term at the point where it is first defined. Such a term belongs in this glossary and its entry links back to that definition. Bold is not used merely for emphasis, audience labels, example names or whole interpretive claims. Short labels inside callouts and margin notes are navigational labels rather than definitions and do not become glossary entries.

When a new reusable mathematical or computational term enters the course, add it here with:

  1. a link to its first definition in the Reader;
  2. a precise definition at the level used in the course;
  3. an applied translation tied to a data or scientific decision; and
  4. one sentence stating where that translation can mislead.

| [Span](topics/topic-02/algebra-survival-guide.qmd#vectors-bases-and-coordinate-columns) | All linear combinations of a specified set of vectors. | Everything that can be assembled from the available building blocks. | A long generating list may contain redundant vectors. |
| [Kernel](topics/topic-02/algebra-survival-guide.qmd#kernel-image-rank-and-nullity) | For $T:V\to W$, the subspace $\{v:T(v)=0\}$. | Input combinations whose effects cancel completely under the map. | Being in a kernel is relative to a particular map. |
| [Image](topics/topic-02/algebra-survival-guide.qmd#kernel-image-rank-and-nullity) | For $T:V\to W$, the subspace $\{T(v):v\in V\}$. | Output patterns that the available inputs can actually produce. | The image need not equal the full codomain. |
| [Rank](topics/topic-02/algebra-survival-guide.qmd#kernel-image-rank-and-nullity) | The dimension of the image of a linear map. | The number of independent output directions produced by the map. | Matrix rank is basis-independent, though the matrix entries are not. |
| [Nullity](topics/topic-02/algebra-survival-guide.qmd#kernel-image-rank-and-nullity) | The dimension of the kernel of a linear map. | The number of independent input directions erased by the map. | A large nullity has meaning only after the domain and map are named. |
| [Rank-nullity theorem](topics/topic-02/algebra-survival-guide.qmd#kernel-image-rank-and-nullity) | For finite-dimensional $V$, $\dim V=\operatorname{rank}T+\operatorname{nullity}T$. | Every independent input direction is either retained in the image or lost to the kernel. | The split is relative to the stated linear map. |
| [Quotient vector space $V/W$](topics/topic-02/algebra-survival-guide.qmd#quotients-one-idea-that-may-be-less-familiar) | Equivalence classes under $v\sim v'$ when $v-v'\in W$. | Treat differences lying in $W$ as irrelevant or already accounted for. | The slash is not numerical division, and a class has many representatives. |
| [Equivalence class $[v]$](topics/topic-02/algebra-survival-guide.qmd#quotients-one-idea-that-may-be-less-familiar) | All elements equivalent to $v$ under a specified relation. | Every representation treated as the same after the agreed differences are ignored. | A displayed representative is not canonical unless additional structure selects it. |
| [Coefficient field](topics/topic-02/algebra-survival-guide.qmd#coefficient-fields) | The field used for chain coefficients, here usually $\mathbb F_2$. | The arithmetic system used to combine and cancel building blocks. | Features can depend on coefficients, especially in the presence of torsion. |
| [Chain space $C_p$](topics/topic-02/homology-detail.qmd#coefficients-and-chain-spaces) | The vector space freely generated by the $p$-simplices. | All formal selections or combinations of $p$-dimensional pieces. | A chain is algebraic bookkeeping, not necessarily one connected geometric object. |
| [Chain complex](topics/topic-02/homology-detail.qmd#chain-complex) | Vector spaces $C_p$ linked by boundary maps with $\partial_{p-1}\partial_p=0$. | The simplicial complex recorded dimension by dimension, together with how each layer meets the one below. | A chain complex is algebraic data derived from $K$; it is not another geometric complex. |
| [Boundary map $\partial_p$](topics/topic-02/homology-detail.qmd#boundary-maps-cycles-and-boundaries) | The linear map sending each $p$-simplex to the signed sum of its faces. | A rule extracting the exposed lower-dimensional boundary of a collection of pieces. | Over $\mathbb F_2$ signs vanish; over other coefficients orientation matters. |
| [Cycle $Z_p$](topics/topic-02/homology-detail.qmd#boundary-maps-cycles-and-boundaries) | A chain in $\ker\partial_p$. | A closed algebraic pattern with no remaining boundary. | A cycle need not represent a nonzero hole; it may itself be a boundary. |
| [Boundary $B_p$](topics/topic-02/homology-detail.qmd#boundary-maps-cycles-and-boundaries) | A chain in $\operatorname{im}\partial_{p+1}$. | A closed pattern already filled by something one dimension higher. | “Boundary” here is algebraic, not merely the visible edge of a plotted region. |
| [Homology $H_p$](topics/topic-02/homology.qmd#homology) | An algebraic invariant of a space; for a simplicial complex it is calculated as the quotient $Z_p/B_p$. | It records connected pieces and independent unfilled cycles in each dimension. | It is coarser than homotopy and depends on the represented space and coefficient system. |
| [Homology class](topics/topic-02/homology-detail.qmd#homology-class) | An equivalence class $[z]=z+B_p$ of cycles that differ by boundaries. | One topological feature represented by any of several cycles with the same unfilled content. | A class is not a unique geometric loop; representative cycles can differ by an available boundary. |
| [Betti number $\beta_p$](topics/topic-02/homology.qmd#betti-number) | The dimension of $H_p$ over the chosen field. | The number of independent $p$-dimensional topological degrees of freedom. | It is a count at one object or scale and contains no feature identity. |
| [Euler characteristic $\chi$](topics/topic-02/homology-detail.qmd#euler-characteristic) | The alternating sum of simplex counts, equivalently Betti numbers for a finite complex. | A compact accounting checksum for the complex. | Different homology can share the same Euler characteristic. |

## Filtrations and persistence

| Mathematical term | Precise meaning | Applied or intuitive translation | Interpretation caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Filtration](topics/topic-03/index.qmd) | A nested family $K_a\subseteq K_b$ for $a\leq b$. | A controlled sweep through a modelling threshold. | The parameter is not automatically physical time. |
| [Filtration function](topics/topic-03/detail.qmd#filtration-functions) | A face-monotone value assigned to every simplex. | The rule deciding when each relation becomes available. | Birth inherits meaning from this rule, not from topology alone. |
| [Čech complex](topics/topic-03/detail.qmd#čech-complexes-and-the-nerve-theorem) | The nerve of equal-radius balls centred on a point cloud. | Record groups whose neighbourhoods share a genuinely common point. | Joint intersection is stronger than pairwise intersection. |
| [Vietoris–Rips complex](topics/topic-03/detail.qmd#vietorisrips-complexes) | The clique complex on pairs below a distance threshold. | Infer higher-order groups from pairwise proximity. | Check whether the parameter denotes radius or pairwise-distance threshold. |
| [Inclusion-induced map](topics/topic-04/index.qmd) | The map $H_p(K_a)\to H_p(K_b)$ induced by $K_a\hookrightarrow K_b$. | The rule describing what an earlier feature becomes after more simplices are added. | The map need not be injective; an old class can map to zero. |
| [Inclusion map](topics/topic-03/detail.qmd#inclusion-maps) | For $K_a\subseteq K_b$, the map that sends each simplex of $K_a$ to the same simplex in $K_b$. | The bookkeeping link that says the earlier complex sits inside the later one. | It is a map of complexes, not yet the induced map between their homology spaces. |
| [Functor](topics/topic-04/detail.qmd#functor-note) | A rule sending objects to objects and maps to maps while preserving identities and composition. | A consistent translation that retains both the states and how one state maps to another. | The word adds no new empirical information; it records a structural compatibility. |
| [Persistence module](index.qmd#the-persistent-homology-construction) | Vector spaces $H_p(K_a)$ together with all compatible maps across scale. | Homology with its cross-scale memory retained. | A sequence of Betti numbers is not a persistence module. |
| [Topological feature](topics/topic-04/index.qmd) | A homology class considered as it appears and persists through a filtration. | A connected component, loop or cavity being followed across scale. | It is an algebraic class, not necessarily one unique visible shape or representative cycle. |
| [Birth](topics/topic-04/summaries.qmd) | The first parameter at which a class appears in the module. | The threshold where a new independent organisation becomes detectable. | It is construction-dependent and may arise from sampling or ties. |
| [Death](topics/topic-04/summaries.qmd) | The parameter where a class merges into an older class or becomes a boundary. | The threshold where that independent organisation is no longer distinguishable. | The underlying chain may still exist after its class dies. |
| [Persistence](topics/topic-04/summaries.qmd) | Death minus birth for a finite interval. | The range of thresholds over which a class remains distinguishable. | Long persistence is not automatically scientific importance. |
| [Interval module](topics/topic-04/summaries-detail.qmd#interval-modules) | A module equal to the field on one interval and zero elsewhere. | One idealised feature that is alive over one parameter range. | The full module is usually a direct sum of many intervals. |
| [Barcode](topics/topic-04/summaries.qmd#two-views-of-the-same-intervals) | The multiset of intervals in an interval decomposition. | A timeline-like display of feature lifetimes across scale. | Classification by barcodes needs suitable one-parameter finiteness or tameness assumptions. |
| [Persistence diagram](topics/topic-04/summaries.qmd#two-views-of-the-same-intervals) | Birth-death points encoding the same interval data. | A scatter plot of when features appear and disappear. | Distance from the diagonal measures persistence, not truth or usefulness. |
| [Representative cycle](topics/topic-02/homology-detail.qmd#boundary-maps-cycles-and-boundaries) | A cycle chosen from a homology class. | One concrete loop or void boundary used to localise an abstract feature. | The choice is generally not unique, so its precise location should not be treated as canonical. |
| [Essential class](topics/topic-04/summaries-detail.qmd#barcode-and-diagram) | A class with no finite death in the available filtration. | Structure that remains after the sweep reaches its endpoint. | It may be genuinely global or merely censored by a truncated parameter range. |
| [Stability](topics/topic-05/index.qmd) | A theorem bounding diagram change by an appropriate change in input or filtration. | Small permitted perturbations cannot arbitrarily move the persistence output. | Stability does not imply interpretability, significance or predictive value. |

## Dynamic data

| Mathematical term | Precise meaning | Applied or intuitive translation | Interpretation caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Snapshot persistence](topics/topic-06/detail.qmd) | Separate static persistence computations at successive times or windows. | A movie of barcode summaries. | Similar-looking bars are not automatically the same tracked feature. |
| [Dynamic point cloud](topics/topic-06/detail.qmd#dynamic-point-clouds) | A family of finite point sets $X_t$ indexed by physical time or another external variable. | A cloud whose observed locations, membership or both change from frame to frame. | The indexing alone supplies no correspondence between points or topological classes. |
| [Betti surface](topics/topic-06/detail.qmd#betti-surfaces-and-crocker-displays) | A function or display of Betti number across external time and filtration scale. | A two-dimensional view of how feature counts vary through time and resolution. | It counts classes but does not track their identities. |
| [Vineyard proxy](topics/topic-06/detail.qmd#diagram-stacks) | A visual stack of independently computed persistence diagrams. | A preliminary view of how diagram points appear to move. | Without justified correspondence it is not a vineyard. |
| [Vineyard](topics/topic-06/index.qmd#vineyards-follow-pairings-along-a-path-of-filtrations) | Trajectories of persistence-diagram points under a changing function. | Feature summaries traced continuously as data evolve. | Matching diagram points is additional structure beyond repeated diagrams. |
| [Zigzag persistence](topics/topic-06/index.qmd#zigzag-persistence-allow-structure-to-enter-and-leave) | Persistence for diagrams whose maps may point forward or backward. | Track structure when observations or complexes can be added and removed. | It solves a different problem from an ordinary increasing filtration. |
| [Multiparameter persistence](topics/topic-06/multiparameter.qmd#the-high-level-idea) | A module indexed by a partial order with two or more parameters. | Retain several genuine modelling axes without collapsing them prematurely. | There is generally no complete barcode classification analogous to the one-parameter case. |
| [Lower-star filtration](topics/topic-03/detail.qmd#lower-star-filtration) | A filtration assigning each simplex the maximum value on its vertices. | Activate a relation when its highest-valued required vertex becomes available. | Negating values changes a low-feature question into a high-feature question. |
| [Bottleneck distance](topics/topic-05/index.qmd#compare) | The minimum possible maximum matching cost between diagrams, allowing diagonal matches. | The size of the single worst discrepancy after optimal matching. | It can ignore the accumulated effect of many smaller changes. |
| [Wasserstein distance](topics/topic-05/index.qmd#compare) | An optimal matching cost that aggregates powered pointwise costs. | Total diagram discrepancy rather than only the worst match. | Its value depends on the order, ground metric and treatment of diagonal matches. |
| [Betti curve](topics/topic-05/index.qmd#transform) | The function counting intervals alive at each filtration value. | A multiscale feature-count profile. | It discards interval identity and can map different diagrams to the same curve. |
| [Persistence landscape](topics/topic-05/index.qmd#transform) | Ordered functions obtained from the triangular functions associated with persistence intervals. | A functional representation of interval prominence across scale. | Truncating levels or discretising the scale axis loses information. |
| [Persistence image](topics/topic-05/index.qmd#transform) | A weighted, smoothed rasterisation of diagram points. | A fixed-length feature array for statistics or machine learning. | Resolution, smoothing and weighting are modelling parameters. |
| [Persistence entropy](topics/topic-05/index.qmd#transform) | Shannon entropy of normalised interval lengths, usually for finite intervals. | A scalar describing how evenly persistence is distributed across features. | It discards birth locations and feature identity, and conventions for essential or zero-length intervals matter. |
| [Maximum persistence](topics/topic-05/index.qmd#transform) | The largest finite lifespan $\max_i(d_i-b_i)$ in a selected diagram. | The lifetime of the single longest-lived class. | It ignores every other class and depends on filtration units. |
| [Total persistence](topics/topic-05/index.qmd#transform) | An aggregate such as $\sum_i(d_i-b_i)^q$ over selected finite intervals. | The accumulated lifetime carried by the diagram. | Its value depends on the exponent, diagram size and treatment of essential intervals. |
| [Null model](topics/topic-05/index.qmd#validation-begins-with-the-claimed-mechanism) | A probability model representing structure expected without the target mechanism. | The comparison world against which “unusual” is defined. | An easy-to-simulate null may preserve the wrong properties. |
| [Surrogate data](topics/topic-05/index.qmd#validation-begins-with-the-claimed-mechanism) | Synthetic or transformed observations generated under a null while preserving selected features. | A controlled negative comparison passed through the same pipeline. | Validity depends on what is preserved, destroyed and exchangeable. |
| [CROCKER-style plot](topics/topic-06/detail.qmd#betti-surfaces-and-crocker-displays) | A display of Betti number or rank across physical time and filtration scale. | A heat map of how multiscale topological counts evolve. | It summarises repeated diagrams and does not track class identity. |
| [SW1PerS](topics/topic-06/sw1pers.qmd#the-high-level-idea) | A sliding-window persistent homology method for quantifying periodicity in signals. | Turn repeated signal phases into a loop-like point cloud and measure its persistence. | Lag, window dimension, sampling and normalisation shape the result; it is not a generic feature-tracking method. |
| [Physical time $t$](topics/topic-06/detail.qmd) | The external index describing when a system is observed. | Clock time, trial progress or window position. | It must not be confused with filtration scale. |
| [Filtration scale $\varepsilon$](topics/topic-03/index.qmd#two-ways-a-filtration-can-arise) | The ordered threshold within one persistence calculation. | The resolution or relation-strength dial used to construct topology. | It is not necessarily a physical unit unless the construction supports that interpretation. |
| [Feature tracking](topics/topic-06/index.qmd) | An explicit correspondence assigning identity to features across an external index. | Saying which evolving feature is the continuation of which earlier feature. | Nearest diagram points or similar lifetimes do not automatically establish identity. |
| [Vineyard module](topics/topic-06/correspondence-detail.qmd#vineyard-versus-vineyard-module) | A time-indexed family of persistence modules with coherent transport maps. | Vines plus the algebra describing how feature directions mix through time. | It is richer than a vineyard and requires additional structure to estimate. |

## Maintenance rule

Bold type in the main Reader prose is reserved for a reusable technical term at the point
where it is first defined. Such a term belongs in this glossary and its entry
links back to that definition. Bold is not used merely for emphasis, audience
labels, example names or whole interpretive claims. Short labels inside
callouts and margin notes are navigational labels rather than definitions and
do not become glossary entries.

When a new reusable mathematical or computational term enters the course, add
it here with:

1. a link to its first definition in the Reader;
2. a precise definition at the level used in the course;
3. an applied translation tied to a data or scientific decision; and
4. one sentence stating where that translation can mislead.
